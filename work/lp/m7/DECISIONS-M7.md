Honest limit recorded: two colluders can both use one leaked copy; damage contained to the traced key. Tardos/Boneh-Shaw overhead buys nothing under unforgeable AEAD. | RESEARCH finding #8 | 1 |
| D-M7-4 | **CCP-M6 (glm1) ruled ACCEPT by M1 owner.** Formula adopted exactly as proposed: `payloadKey = HKDF-SHA256(ikm=sessionKey, salt=fromHex(build_hash) ‖ s, info="payload-key" ‖ fromHex(watermark_id), L=32)` where `s` = the stub's per-fetch value, forwarded by the init as an OPTIONAL base64url JSON field `s` (0..64 bytes decoded) in the `/auth/:id/payload` request. Choice rationale (M1's pick per CCP): body field over `X-Fetch-Value` header — matches the existing `{v, session_token, payload_ref}` body style; some executor `request` implementations surface limited header control while all pass JSON bodies. Empty/absent `s` ⇒ salt degrades to `fromHex(build_hash)` (byte-identical to today — backward compatible by construction, M3 client side lands on glm1's ruling notice). Wrong `s` ⇒ payload AEAD open fails (client sees generic failure, no oracle). | CCP-M6, RESEARCH finding #2 | 2 (cross-module, ruled; M3 side = glm1 per CCP) |
